# What makes a carousel work

Read this before writing the copy for any carousel. It comes from studying carousels that did well (hundreds of
thousands of views, thousands of saves) and the notes their creator gave on every round of them.

Two kinds of rules live here, and they are not equal:
- **Principles** hold in every look and every voice. They are why a carousel gets swiped, saved and shared.
- **Defaults** are one good way to do it. The buyer's own look (`look.md`, its "Standing notes") and their notes
  beat every default. A buyer whose style is all lowercase, one sentence per slide, no labels, no CTA keyword, gets
  exactly that, done well. Never "correct" their style back to a default.

## Principles

1. **The swipe is earned, slide by slide.** Slide 1 makes a specific promise. Every slide after it pays some of it
   off AND makes the next swipe worth it. A slide that does neither gets cut or merged.
2. **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs "and" in its headline, it is two slides (or one of them goes).
3. **The headline says the point, not the topic.** "One command to one shot your project." beats "Tip 1:
   autonomy". "Your navbar looks like a template." beats "Navbars". The label (TIP 01, STEP 2, SITE 04) carries the
   position, so the headline does not have to.
4. **Make it worth keeping.** Carousels win on saves and sends. Give people something they will come back for: the
   prompt to paste, the exact steps, the price to charge, the list of tools, the script, the math. Mark it clearly
   ("Paste this", "How to pitch it", "Say this instead") and keep it short enough to read on a phone.
5. **Readable on a phone, at a glance.** A slide is read in about two seconds at the size of a thumb. One loud
   thing, then support, then small print. If you squint at the contact sheet and cannot tell what the slide is
   about, neither can a scroller.
6. **Real or clearly a placeholder.** Commands, links, prices, names, numbers and quotes are checked before they go
   on a slide (search for anything you are not sure of). Never invented results, testimonials, follower counts or
   screenshots of things that did not happen. A quote from a real person is their real words, credited.
7. **Their voice, their words.** Write the way the buyer talks (their brand voice if they have one, the words they
   used in their video when the carousel comes from one). A carousel that sounds like a press release is dead.

## The arc

| Slide | Its job | What usually goes on it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | Stop the scroll, promise a payoff | the claim or the number, one quiet line saying what is inside ("Swipe for 8 copy-paste fixes") |
| 2 Stakes (optional) | Make them care before the list starts | why it matters, the problem, old way vs new way, the math |
| Middle | Pay it off, one idea each | headline (the point), one line of why, the thing to keep, a punchline |
| Last | One action | what to do and what they get: comment a word, save it, send it to someone, follow for part 2 |

- **How many slides:** as many as the content needs. 5 to 10 is the usual range; a tight 5 beats a padded 9. A
  list of 8 tips is 1 + (1) + 8 + 1.
- **The last slide never adds new content.** It asks for one thing. A keyword CTA says what they get ("Comment
  DESIGN and I'll send you all 8 links"). The follow line is secondary and small.
- **Slide 2 is where weak carousels lose people.** If the hook is strong, slide 2 raises the stakes or shows the
  shift (old vs new, the cost of not knowing, the surprising number). It is not a table of contents.

## Slide 1, the hook

- **Specific beats clever.** A number, an outcome, a named tool or audience: "8 free sites that make Claude a
  design genius", "How to build and sell $500 websites to hit $10K per month", "The closest thing to hiring a full
  staff for $0".
- **Loud is the action and the outcome. Quiet is the glue.** In the $500 websites hook, "BUILD + SELL" and
  "$10K PER MONTH" were loud; "$500 websites to hit" was a small serif line between them. At most about 3 loud
  lines; the connecting words go quiet (`((...))` in the headline).
- **One accent.** The word or phrase that hits hardest gets the accent colour. Accent everything and nothing pops.
- **Say what is inside.** A small line under or after the headline: "Swipe for 6 services and what to charge",
  "Everything you need to get the most out of the new model".
- Hook shapes that work as text on a slide: "[Tool] just killed [job]", "[Number] [things] for [tool]", "You need
  to understand the [number] levels of [thing]", "[Number] things to do the moment you start [thing]", "You haven't
  unlocked [capability] yet", "Don't say [common phrase], say [better phrase]", "You can now replace [team] with
  [tool]", "Stop [common mistake]", "[Big result] in [short time]".
- Shapes that need a voice and a face (leave them to reels): POV and meme formats, replies to comments, rants,
  "watch this" speed demos, news that is stale in a week.
- Contrast is the engine of most strong carousels: wrong vs right, myth vs truth, beginner vs pro, before vs after,
  what people see vs what it takes, the old way vs the new way. Name the contrast the carousel runs on before you
  write it; it decides the hook and slide 2.

## Middle slides

- **Headline:** the point, 2 to 4 short lines, the key phrase accented. A claim, an instruction, or a surprising
  fact.
- **Sub line:** one sentence (two at most) on why it matters or what it does. Its payoff phrase can take the accent.
- **The thing to keep:** in its own block with a short label. Prompts and scripts stay under about 150 characters
  on a slide; a longer one goes in the free resource the CTA sends.
- **Punchline (optional):** one line at the bottom that makes it stick. "A junior marketer costs $4K a month. This
  costs nothing." "Done and shown beats perfect and hidden."
- **A series stays a series.** Eight tips can share one layout (the change from slide to slide is the content).
  When the KIND of slide changes (a list, then the math, then a comparison), the layout changes with it.
- **Drama is for the hook and one or two beats.** The setup-small, punchline-huge treatment on every slide flattens
  all of them.
- **Parallel shape, fresh words.** Each middle slide follows the same pattern (label, point, why, keep), with its
  own concrete words.

## Picking the right layout for the content

| The content is | Use (see slides.md) |
|---|---|
| 3 to 5 short points | `items` bullets, plus a `callout` |
| named things with a line each (tools, sites, people) | `items` rows |
| ordered steps | `items` steps |
| options where one is the pick | `items` grid with one `hot` |
| what changed, word for word | `items` compare (struck old, arrow, new) |
| the old way vs the new way, side by side | `items` versus |
| one number that is the point | `big` |
| someone's words | `quote` |
| a prompt, a command, a script, a message to send | `payload` |
| a website, an app, a result, a frame from their video | `image` (frame, bleed or strip) |
| price chips or tags under a hook | `items` chips |

## Hierarchy on a slide

- **Three levels:** loud (the headline, one per slide), support (sub line, items, payload), quiet (labels, handle,
  counter, swipe cue). The jump from loud to support is big: a headline about 2.5 to 4 times the body size.
- **Accent colour is for meaning:** the hit word in the headline, the payoff in the sub line, the thing to keep.
- **Fill the canvas.** No dead zones: the headline group sits at the top, the payoff at the bottom, the middle grows
  to fill (the renderer does this). A slide that still looks empty needs a visual or should merge with the next.
- **Text over a picture** gets a dark or light fade behind it. A screenshot is shown small and clean, with its
  label above it, never covering it. Two pictures to compare sit side by side, not stacked.
- **Chrome stays quiet and consistent:** handle, slide counter, swipe cue. The cue is hidden on the last slide.

## Photos

In a photo-led look the photo IS the hook: the face, the place, the vibe stop the scroll, and the words ride on it.
- **The cover gets the best photo of the person:** eye contact or a strong expression, sharp, good light, and calm
  space (sky, wall, floor) where the headline goes. Text never covers a face.
- **One idea per photo, too.** A slide about the morning routine shows the morning; a slide about the result shows
  the result. A photo that only decorates can go.
- **One consistent set:** same light and colour across the carousel (or a deliberate before/after contrast). Mixing
  a dark indoor shot with a bright beach shot reads as random.
- **Vary the framing:** close face, half body, detail (hands, coffee, laptop), wide place. A collage earns its place
  when the point is "a lot happened" (a day, a trip, a launch); otherwise one strong photo beats four small ones.
- **Text on photos:** sit it on the calm part of the frame; the look's fade or card keeps it readable. Fewer words on
  photo slides than on text slides.
- Real photos over generated ones whenever the buyer has them: people follow people. Generated pictures are for
  scenes, objects and moods, never for fake results or a fake life.

## Writing for reading

- Short words, short lines, numbers as digits ($500, 8, 10 minutes).
- No corporate words (leverage, seamless, innovative, robust, unlock your potential, game-changer).
- No em dashes on slides. Periods and commas. (It is also the clearest tell of AI-written copy.)
- No hashtags or emojis on slides unless the buyer's look uses them.
- Cut every word the slide survives without. Then read it out loud once: it should sound like the buyer.
- Caps or sentence case is the look's choice. The copy is written in normal case either way: the look decides.

## From a video to a carousel

A reel is heard; a carousel is read. The idea, the order and the buyer's best lines stay; the talking goes.
- The hook of the reel usually makes the hook of the carousel, tightened for reading.
- Every point the reel makes becomes a middle slide; examples and asides become the sub line or get cut.
- Their memorable phrasings go on the slides word for word (they are the voice). The filler ("so basically",
  "what you want to do is") never does.
- The reel's CTA keyword stays the same on the last slide, so both posts point to one resource.
- A frame from the video makes a strong hook or a proof slide. Pick frames with open eyes, a clear expression and
  no motion blur; a screen recording frame works as an `image` with a label.
- references/from-video.md has the steps.

## Checking the result (every render)

Look at the contact sheet, then at any slide the check flagged, at full size:
- Can you tell what every slide is about from the sheet alone?
- Is there one loud thing per slide? Is the accent used on meaning only?
- Does every middle slide give something worth keeping?
- Any text too small, cut off, past the margin or crowding the handle? (The check lines say so; fix by cutting
  words or splitting the slide, not by shrinking type below readable.)
- Same idea twice? Merge. A slide with no job? Cut.
